Justice and Empowerment of Minorities

Hate crimes against religious minorities have become increasingly common in India. Report after report have highlighted the rise of violent incidents against the minorities in India. The recent data shows that among the minorities hate crimes against Muslims have the largest volume with a 300% increase since the right-wing government came to power in the center in 2014. One of them is Jamiat Ulama-i- Hind, the oldest and largest socio-cultural organization of Indian Muslims, which has been on the ground for long, combating hate crimes committed against the country's religious minorities.

And in this struggle, their justice and empowerment initiative hopes to change the disturbing trends of the increasing hate crimes against religious minorities. With the mission of safeguarding their human rights and countering hate speech targeted against them, JEM aims to ensure the implementation of equal rights, justice, peace, religious tolerance and coexistence for all in the country. Their objectives include developing a hate crime database, promoting the rule of law and access to justice, upholding citizens security and human rights, defending religious minorities and marginalized communities and promoting peace-building and interfaith harmony.

These objectives tie with our ultimate goal of empowering the oppressed and wrongly persecuted and promoting a more just and inclusive society in India. For all of this to come together, JEM has four departments - Hate Crime Response Unit, Case Review Committee, Media Unit and Legal Unit. And this is how our work channels through them. It begins with the organization's reporting and ground networks when news about hate crimes is first received.

We believe in verifying cases ourselves rather than relying solely on secondary sources. For this second step, local members of our Hate Crime Response Unit are deployed on the ground to provide immediate aid and collect testimonies from the victims. We also have our own website, a form to report a hate crime and a helpline number. All the received and collected information is then verified, analyzed and further listed under one of the seven types of cases that we are equipped to handle, namely acts of hate, acts of violence, attacks on religious places, police atrocities, discrimination, exclusion and prejudice, state-sponsored discriminatory practices and media manipulation and distortion of facts.

At JEM, a specialized case review committee carefully screens these cases to assess how to help with our limited resources. And this is the final and most crucial part, intervention, which can fall into either of the four umbrella categories - reporting, documentation, advocacy and training and capacity building. With our diligent reporting, we bring forth policy demands, appeal to figureheads and dignitaries by writing letters and grab the media spotlight on these otherwise ignored issues.

With facts out there, we continue with meticulous documentation of each and every case. We maintain a hate crime database, which documents alarming trends of hate crimes and simultaneously aids in developing tailored solutions to combat these crimes. In this regard, we also publish four quarterly reviews and an annual review every year. All of this documentation offers evidence for our long-term strategy for intervention, which is our advocacy for better legislation and policy making at different levels.

Some of our legally advocated interventions have had a significant positive impact on the outcome of cases. At JEM, we also believe in capacity-building and empowering people. For this, we aim to establish state, city and town units in as many locations as possible. Well-versed in social political dynamics, these units are trained to promptly identify and address hate crimes and build trust and solidarity between different communities.

They play a critical role in countering hate crimes by providing local presence, immediate assistance to victims and connecting them with legal and victim assistance services. Through their tireless efforts, JEM works towards empowering victims, promoting equality and fostering peace in India. Community support is crucial in the endeavor to counter hate crimes and promote a more inclusive society. Together, we can stand against discrimination and violence, ensuring that every individual feels safe and protected, regardless of their religious or social background.

By supporting organizations like JEM and actively participating in initiatives, promoting dialogue, understanding and harmony, we can create a society where tolerance and acceptance prevail. Let us join hands and become champions for justice, equality and peace, supporting the good fight against hate.

About JEM

Documentation, on the record.

JEM is an initiative of the Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind, the country's oldest and largest socio-cultural organisation of Indian Muslims. It collects, collates and presents cases of harassment perpetrated against the country's minorities — promoting rule of law, access to justice, equal rights and citizens' security. Alongside this documentation JEM publishes Monthly Reports, four Quarterly Reviews and an Annual Review of the record.

About JEM → See our work →

Four Quarterly Reviews and an Annual Review are published every year, documenting hate crimes against minorities and JEM's response — besides working to ensure equal rights, justice, religious tolerance and coexistence in the country.
